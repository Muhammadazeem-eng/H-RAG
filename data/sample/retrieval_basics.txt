Retrieval-Augmented Generation, commonly abbreviated RAG, is a paradigm in
which a language model is conditioned on documents retrieved at inference
time from an external knowledge source. The retrieval step gives the model
access to information that may not be present in its training data and helps
reduce hallucinations on knowledge-intensive tasks.

A typical RAG pipeline has three stages: document ingestion, retrieval, and
generation. During ingestion, source documents are split into chunks and
indexed in a vector store or lexical index. During retrieval, the user query
is encoded and used to fetch the most relevant chunks. During generation, the
retrieved chunks are concatenated with the user query and supplied to a
language model that produces the final answer.

Dense retrieval uses neural embeddings to measure semantic similarity between
queries and documents, so paraphrased queries can still match the right
passages. Sparse retrieval, often based on BM25, scores documents by exact
keyword overlap weighted by inverse document frequency. Hybrid retrievers
combine the two signals, typically with a tunable interpolation weight, to
benefit from both semantic recall and lexical precision.

Multi-turn conversational RAG introduces additional challenges: user
questions can contain pronouns, follow-up references, or implicit topic
shifts. These context-dependent queries are commonly handled by a query
rewriter that transforms them into standalone, retrieval-ready questions
before they reach the retriever.
