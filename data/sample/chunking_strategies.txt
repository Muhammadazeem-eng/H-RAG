Choosing a chunking strategy is one of the most consequential decisions in
designing a retrieval pipeline. A chunk that is too small loses local
context; a chunk that is too large dilutes relevance signals and inflates
generator input.

Fixed-token chunking splits documents on a token budget regardless of
sentence boundaries. It is simple but can fragment ideas mid-clause.
Sentence-window chunking groups N consecutive sentences with a stride S,
creating overlapping chunks that preserve sentence-level coherence while
still being short enough for precise retrieval.

Hierarchical or parent-child chunking takes this further by indexing only
the small child chunks but tracking which parent document each chunk
belongs to. At query time the retriever scores child chunks, but the
generator receives the parent. This pattern - retrieve small, generate
large - has become a standard for long-document RAG.

H-RAG follows the parent-child design with overlapping three-sentence
windows and a stride of two. The choice is unsupervised: no learned chunker
is required, only a sentence splitter and a sliding-window iterator.
